# Draft tutor rules: Choosing redox-identification methods

## Review status

Draft extracted from one short lesson transcript. The tutor should review transcription ambiguities, especially “for the dots,” “if they don't satisfy,” and “animated one,” before approving use. (3.md)

## Student-request types and recognition cues

- **Choosing a method to identify redox:** Recognise this request when a student asks how to know whether to use oxidation state or electron transfer “to determine whether a reaction is redox.” (3.md)
- **Unspecified-method questions:** If the question “doesn't specify for you to use a specific method,” the student may use whichever method they want. (3.md)
- **Questions specifying oxygen transfer:** In Paper 2, recognise that questions will “usually” specify oxygen transfer; the tutor describes this as “the most common one.” (3.md)
- **Reactions involving ions:** When the reaction contains ions, electron transfer may be the easiest method; the tutor gives “I minus become I2” as an example. (3.md)
- **Reactions containing no H or O:** If a specific equation contains neither H nor O, hydrogen transfer and oxygen transfer cannot be used for that equation. (3.md)

## Tutor response approaches

- **Start with permission when no method is prescribed:** Explain that if the question does not specify a particular method, “Just use whatever method you want.” (3.md)
- **Recall the available methods before selecting one:** The tutor introduces four methods: oxidation state, electron transfer, hydrogen transfer, and oxygen transfer. (3.md)
- **Give a practical selection rule:** “You choose a method that is more suited to the scenario that you are given.” (3.md)
- **Present oxidation state as the default/easiest flexible option:** Use the tutor’s “cheat code” framing: “you just use the one that's easiest for you.” (3.md)
- **Match the method to visible species in the equation:** Oxygen transfer requires oxygen to occur as a reactant or product; “Same for hydrogen.” Electron transfer is especially easy where ions are present. (3.md)
- **Use a concrete counterexample to eliminate unsuitable methods:** For an equation without H or O, state that hydrogen transfer and oxygen transfer cannot be used “simply because this reaction doesn't contain H or O.” (3.md)

## Required and preferred vocabulary

- **“Oxidation state” / “O_S_”:** The tutor uses both the full term and the abbreviation “O_S_.” (3.md)
- **“Electron transfer,” “hydrogen transfer,” and “oxygen transfer”:** Use these names for the alternative methods. (3.md)
- **“Oxidised”:** Use when discussing whether a substance has undergone oxidation. (3.md)
- **“Reactant or product”:** Use this wording when checking whether oxygen is present for oxygen-transfer reasoning. (3.md)
- **“Contains ions”:** Use as the cue for when electron-transfer reasoning may be easier. (3.md)
- **“Universal”:** The tutor characterises oxidation state as “universal.” (3.md)
- **“Cheat code”:** An informal phrase the tutor uses when introducing oxidation state as the easiest choice. (3.md)

## Wording to avoid or qualify

- **Do not tell students that a particular method is mandatory if the question has not specified one:** The tutor says, “Just use whatever method you want.” (3.md)
- **Qualify oxygen-transfer and hydrogen-transfer methods by the equation’s contents:** Oxygen transfer is “only applicable” where oxygen occurs in the reaction, and the tutor says “Same for hydrogen.” (3.md)
- **Do not apply hydrogen-transfer or oxygen-transfer reasoning to an equation containing neither H nor O:** The tutor explicitly rules these out in that situation. (3.md)
- **Avoid retaining the initial MCQ-only condition:** The tutor begins “If it's MCQ” and immediately retracts it: “okay no sorry, let me take that back.” (3.md)

## Misconceptions to pre-empt

- **Misconception: there is one compulsory method for every redox-identification question.** Correct by stating that if the question does not specify a method, the student may use whichever method they want. (3.md)
- **Misconception: oxygen transfer can be used for any reaction.** Correct by checking whether oxygen is present as a reactant or product. (3.md)
- **Misconception: hydrogen transfer can be used for an equation with no hydrogen.** Correct by noting that hydrogen transfer cannot be used if the reaction does not contain H. (3.md)
- **Misconception: electron transfer is equally convenient in every equation.** Correct by explaining that it is especially easy when ions are present. (3.md)
- **Misconception: an equation with no H or O can be assessed using hydrogen or oxygen transfer.** Correct by ruling out both methods and choosing a method suited to the scenario. (3.md)
