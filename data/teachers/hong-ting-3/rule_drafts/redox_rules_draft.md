# Draft tutor rules: Redox

## Student-request types and recognition cues

- **Redox-reaction explanation:** Recognise questions such as “use oxidation state to explain why this reaction is a redox reaction.” The response should identify the relevant element, state how its oxidation state changes, and name the compounds in which the initial and final oxidation states occur. (redox.md)
- **Oxidising-agent explanation:** Recognise questions such as “explain why compound X is an oxidising agent.” Treat this as a standard question for which a reusable oxidation-state-based answer structure can be applied. (redox.md)
- **Short-term syllabus prioritisation:** For a student trying to learn the O-level Chemistry syllabus independently in three months, present redox as a topic worth practising early because its questions are standard and students can score well if they put in time and effort; do not describe it as necessarily one of the highest-weightage topics. (redox.md)

## Tutor response approaches

- Teach redox in this order: begin with oxidation state rules, practise standard explanations based on oxidation-state changes, and then cover electron, hydrogen, and oxygen transfer. (redox.md)
- For an oxidation-state explanation, state that the element is oxidised or reduced, give the direction and numerical change in oxidation state, and identify the specific compound associated with each oxidation state. (redox.md)
- Use a reusable sentence structure such as: “Element X is oxidised because its oxidation state increased from plus 1 in XCl to plus 2 in XO.” (redox.md)
- Emphasise both parts needed in the explanation: the oxidation-state number changed, and each number belongs to the element in a specific compound. (redox.md)

## Required and preferred vocabulary

- Use the terms **redox**, **oxidised**, **oxidising agent**, and **oxidation state** when answering the standard question types identified in the lesson. (redox.md)
- State oxidation states numerically and give their direction of change, for example, “increased from plus 1 … to plus 2.” (redox.md)
- Name the compounds containing the element before and after the change, for example, “plus 1 in XCl” and “plus 2 in XO.” (redox.md)
- Use the mnemonic **OIL RIG** when teaching electron transfer in redox. (redox.md)

## Wording to avoid or qualify

- Do not merely say that an element’s oxidation state “increased” or “decreased”; include the initial and final oxidation-state numbers. (redox.md)
- Do not give oxidation-state numbers without identifying the specific compounds in which the element has those oxidation states. (redox.md)
- Do not claim that redox is one of the most important or highest-weightage O-level Chemistry topics. Qualify the recommendation by saying it is a fundamental topic with standard questions that students can learn to score well in through practice. (redox.md)

## Topic map and reusable explanations

- Redox is a fundamental concept needed for understanding harder topics such as electrochemistry. (redox.md)
- Begin redox revision with oxidation state rules. (redox.md)
- Connect oxidation state rules to standard applications: explaining why a reaction is redox and why a compound is an oxidising agent. (redox.md)
- Reuse the structure: “Element X is oxidised because its oxidation state increased from plus 1 in XCl to plus 2 in XO.” Adapt the element, oxidation states, compounds, and oxidation/reduction term to the reaction being discussed. (redox.md)
- Teach students to identify and understand redox through oxidation states as well as electron, hydrogen, and oxygen transfer. (redox.md)

## Analogies and examples to reuse

- Reuse the model example: “Element X is oxidised because its oxidation state increased from plus 1 in XCl to plus 2 in XO.” Use it to show that a complete explanation includes the element, direction of change, numerical oxidation states, and compounds. (redox.md)
- Use **OIL RIG** as the mnemonic associated with electron transfer. (redox.md)

## Misconceptions to pre-empt

- Pre-empt incomplete oxidation-state explanations that mention only that a number increased or decreased. Require the actual initial and final oxidation states and the compounds in which they occur. (redox.md)
- Pre-empt treating oxidation states as the only way to understand redox; also address electron, hydrogen, and oxygen transfer. (redox.md)

## Syllabus boundaries

- Pitch the material at O-level Chemistry. (redox.md)
- Treat electrochemistry as a harder downstream topic that depends on redox understanding, rather than expanding the redox lesson into a detailed electrochemistry lesson. (redox.md)
- Present redox as a productive topic for focused revision, not as a claim about topic weightage or overall importance in the syllabus. (redox.md)
