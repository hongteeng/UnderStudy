# Draft tutor rules: Redox agents and simultaneous oxidation/reduction

## Student-request types and recognition cues

- Recognise conceptual “why” questions about redox-agent changes, such as “why is an oxidizing agent always reduced.” (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Treat a question that names a reaction as redox as requiring an explanation that both reduction and oxidation occur. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)

## Tutor response approaches

- For a conceptual redox question, begin by establishing the core principle: “if a reaction is redox,” “there must be both reduction and oxidation happening.” (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Explain the simultaneous nature of redox informally before giving the conclusion: “you cannot have one guy being reduced and then don’t want to be oxidised.” (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Qualify the explanation for reactions involving more than two reactants: one reactant may be reduced, one oxidised, and another may have no change; alternatively, different elements within one reactant may be reduced and oxidised. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Define an oxidising agent through what it does to another substance: “I cause other people to be oxidised.” (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Define a reducing agent through what it does to another substance: “I cause another person to be reduced.” (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Close by restating the governing principle: “Redox will always have a reduction and oxidation happening.” (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)

## Required and preferred vocabulary

- Use **redox**, **reduction**, and **oxidation** when explaining that both processes occur in a redox reaction. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Use **oxidizing agent** for a substance that “cause[s] other people to be oxidised.” (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Use **reducing agent** for a substance that “cause[s] another person to be reduced.” (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Use the paired conclusion: “a reducing agent will always be oxidized.” (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)

## Wording to avoid or qualify

- When discussing reactions with three reactants, do not imply every reactant must be oxidised or reduced; the tutor notes that a third reactant may have no change. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- When discussing a single reactant containing multiple elements, qualify that one element may be reduced while another is oxidised. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Avoid explaining redox as though reduction can happen alone or oxidation can happen alone; state that both must happen in a redox reaction. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Do not use “oxidizing agent” and “reducing agent” without explaining that the terms refer to what the agent causes another substance to undergo. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)

## Topic map and reusable explanations

- **Redox reactions:** A redox reaction has both reduction and oxidation happening. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- **Two-reactant framing:** If one reactant is reduced, the other reactant is oxidised. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- **More-than-two-reactant qualification:** With three reactants, one may be reduced, one oxidised, and another unchanged. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- **Within-one-reactant qualification:** One element in a reactant may be reduced while another element in that reactant is oxidised. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- **Agent definitions:** An oxidising agent causes another substance to be oxidised; a reducing agent causes another substance to be reduced. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- **Reducing-agent consequence:** If an agent causes another substance to be reduced, the agent itself is oxidised, because reduction and oxidation both occur in redox. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- **Conclusion:** “So a reducing agent will always be oxidized.” (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)

## Analogies and examples to reuse

- Use the informal paired-participant analogy: “you cannot have one guy being reduced and then don’t want to be oxidised. It doesn’t work like that.” (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Use the three-reactant example to show that not every reactant necessarily changes: “one guy reduced, one guy oxidised and the other guy nothing happens.” (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)

## Misconceptions to pre-empt

- Pre-empt the idea that a redox reaction can involve reduction without oxidation, or oxidation without reduction. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Pre-empt the idea that all reactants must change oxidation state in a reaction containing three reactants. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- Pre-empt the idea that only separate reactants can undergo oxidation and reduction; the tutor notes that different elements within one reactant can undergo the two changes. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)

## Syllabus boundaries

- The source addresses conceptual redox explanations involving oxidation, reduction, oxidising agents, and reducing agents. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
- The source does not demonstrate calculations, oxidation-number methods, half-equations, or balancing procedures; do not infer a teaching approach for these from this lesson. (oxidising_agent_reduced.md)
