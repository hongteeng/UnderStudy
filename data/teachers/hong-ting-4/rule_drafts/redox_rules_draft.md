# Draft tutor rules: Redox

## Student-request types and recognition cues

- **Explaining why a reaction is redox:** recognise prompts such as “use oxidation state to explain why this reaction is a redox reaction.” (`redox.md`)
- **Explaining why a compound is an oxidizing agent:** recognise prompts such as “explain why compound X is an oxidizing agent.” The tutor treats this as a standard redox question. (`redox.md`)

## Tutor response approaches

- For an oxidation-state explanation, identify the element, state that it is oxidized or reduced, give its oxidation state before and after, explicitly say whether the number increased or decreased, and identify the specific compound containing the element at each stage. (`redox.md`)
- Teach oxidation-state rules before applying them to standard redox explanation questions. (`redox.md`)
- After oxidation-state identification, cover electron, hydrogen, and oxygen transfer using the mnemonic “oil rig.” (`redox.md`)
- Use a standard answer structure that students can reuse for oxidation-state questions. (`redox.md`)

## Required and preferred vocabulary

- Use the terms **oxidation state**, **oxidized**, **redox reaction**, and **oxidizing agent**. (`redox.md`)
- Explicitly state that the oxidation-state number **increased** when explaining oxidation. (`redox.md`)
- Give both the numerical oxidation states and the compounds in which the element has those states, following the pattern: “element X is oxidized because its oxidation state increased from plus 1 in XCl to plus 2 in XO.” (`redox.md`)
- Use the mnemonic **“oil rig”** when teaching electron transfer alongside hydrogen and oxygen transfer. (`redox.md`)

## Wording to avoid or qualify

- Do not merely say that an element is oxidized; include that its oxidation state **increased**, with the relevant numbers. (`redox.md`)
- Do not give oxidation-state numbers without identifying the specific compounds in which the element has those oxidation states. (`redox.md`)
- Do not describe redox as necessarily the most important or highest-weightage O-Level topic; the tutor recommends it because students can score well in it with time and effort. (`redox.md`)

## Topic map and reusable explanations

- Begin with oxidation-state rules, then apply them to identifying and explaining redox reactions and oxidizing agents. (`redox.md`)
- Reusable oxidation explanation: “element X is oxidized because its oxidation state increased from plus 1 in XCl to plus 2 in XO.” (`redox.md`)
- Link oxidation-state reasoning with electron, hydrogen, and oxygen transfer, using “oil rig” as the mnemonic. (`redox.md`)
- Present redox as a fundamental concept needed for understanding harder topics such as electrochemistry. (`redox.md`)
- Emphasise that redox questions are standard enough for students to learn reusable answer structures. (`redox.md`)

## Analogies and examples to reuse

- Use the generic change from XCl to XO to model a complete oxidation-state explanation: X has oxidation state plus 1 in XCl and plus 2 in XO, so its oxidation state increased and X was oxidized. (`redox.md`)

## Misconceptions to pre-empt

- Pre-empt the idea that naming oxidation alone is a complete explanation: students must state that the oxidation state increased and provide its initial and final values. (`redox.md`)
- Pre-empt incomplete answers that omit the substances involved: students must identify the specific compound containing the element before and after the change. (`redox.md`)
- Do not imply that the tutor’s recommended study order represents topic weightage or absolute importance. It represents topics the tutor believes students can score well in through focused effort. (`redox.md`)

## Syllabus boundaries

- The stated level is O-Level Chemistry. (`redox.md`)
- The lesson’s immediate scope is redox: oxidation-state rules, standard oxidation-state explanations, oxidizing agents, and electron, hydrogen, and oxygen transfer. (`redox.md`)
- Electrochemistry is mentioned only as a harder topic that depends on understanding redox. (`redox.md`)
- The lesson does not claim that redox has the highest examination weightage or is the most important topic. (`redox.md`)
